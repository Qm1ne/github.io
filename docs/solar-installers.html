    What the paperwork costs you now

    A photovoltaic application is the same information written out many times over: the client, the site, the panel and inverter models, the sizing figures, the references that tie the drawings together. The survey is the easy part. Turning it into a complete file is where the afternoon goes.

    • Up to four hours per file, most of it retyping numbers someone already wrote down on site.
    • One figure copied across wrongly travels into every document that references it.
    • The file waits for an afternoon that nobody in the office has, so the installation date moves.

    How it works

    Three steps, and the only one that needs your judgement is the last.

    1. 1

      Send the survey data

      Client, location, panel and inverter models, the sizing figures. The same information that comes back from a site visit, entered once.

    2. 2

      The workflow writes the pack

      It checks the figures, runs the calculations and writes every document from them in the format STEG expects. One run covers a pack of around ten documents.

    3. 3

      You check it and submit

      The pack comes back ready to read. Check it against the site you visited, sign it, and file it with STEG yourself.

    A person signs it, not a model

    What comes out is a draft. A photovoltaic application is a government document, and a government document gets read by somebody who knows the site before it is submitted. The workflow does the typing and the arithmetic. What gets filed stays your responsibility.

    Questions installers ask first

    What does it cost, and how soon does it run?+

    We quote per build after a call. The price follows how many documents your pack contains and how your survey data arrives today: a form, a spreadsheet or paper. You get the figure in writing after the call.

    Where does our client data go?+

    Survey data and client details pass through the workflow and the language model that writes the documents. Where it is hosted is part of the build: your own server and storage, or ours. Bring whatever your client contracts require to the call and we build to that.

    Who checks the file before it reaches STEG?+

    You do. Nothing is submitted automatically and the workflow has no route to STEG. It produces the pack. Someone in your office reads it and files it.

    What if STEG sends the file back?+

    Correct the source figure and run it again, which takes minutes rather than another afternoon. If the rejection is about a changed requirement rather than your data, that is a template update on our side and the next run picks it up.
